## Preliminary structural findings

Public evidence repeatedly shows acquisition through Facebook/TikTok/Instagram and conversion through WhatsApp, often combined with physical stores, catalog links and national shipping.

Observed examples include:
- IRIS COMPUTER: Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Discord, Kick and multiple sales advisors in Santa Cruz.
- JC Computers: Facebook, TikTok, WhatsApp catalog, physical stores in Santa Cruz, multiple sales contacts, national shipping.
- CYREX Store: Facebook, TikTok, WhatsApp community, branches in Cochabamba and Santa Cruz.
- BGAMER.PRO: ecommerce, WhatsApp group, physical store in Santa Cruz, national shipping.
- Gamer Lab: La Paz, WhatsApp, national shipping, product positioning by gaming, architecture, engineering, CAD, design, editing, AI and professional use.
- Origins Store: La Paz/El Alto, WhatsApp, ecommerce, products, national shipping and warranty claims.
- TL Gaming catalog: physical stores in Sucre/Oruro plus national shipping and payment-on-delivery in several other cities.

Conclusion: social + WhatsApp + physical proof + national logistics is a common competitive pattern; IPCENTER cannot model Bolivia as isolated city markets.

## Demand / friction signals from public conversations
These are anecdotal public observations, NOT population prevalence.

Recurring signal classes:

### TRUST / FRAUD RISK
- Buyers actively seek physical-location verification and distrust unusually low prices.
- A March 2026 public discussion about a supposed Oruro laptop store produced multiple independent reports that the location did not exist and that WhatsApp contact blocked buyers who attempted in-person verification.
- Recent La Paz conversations describe concern over fake/original accessories and consumer recourse.

### WARRANTY / POST-SALE
- Buyers explicitly compare local purchase vs foreign import based on warranty, not only price.
- A September 2026 La Paz discussion asks how expensive-component warranty claims are handled and expresses fear that a shop may blame the buyer or mishandle a defective component.

### PRICE + IMPORT COMPLEXITY
- Public conversations repeatedly compare local price, US price, importers, Amazon, dollar availability, customs and logistics.
- Buyers often do not know total landed cost and ask whether importing is actually cheaper.

### EXACT VARIANT / AVAILABILITY
- Buyers search for specific GPU/motherboard/laptop variants rather than generic categories.
- Public discussion indicates local stock may be concentrated in certain brands while specific alternatives are harder to find.

### PRODUCT FIT / USE CASE
- Architecture students explicitly ask what laptop configuration is needed for AutoCAD, SketchUp, Revit and mobility through a full degree.
- Current competitors already advertise by use case: architecture, engineering, CAD, 3D rendering, content creation, AI, programming, gaming, study and professional work.

### NATIONAL DELIVERY / CITY DEMAND
- People ask whether stores ship to Potosí, La Paz, Cochabamba, Santa Cruz and other departments.
- Major competitors advertise national shipping, multiple-city branches or payment-on-delivery outside their home city.

## Important competitor correction
Generic "Product Fit" is not an empty market space. Competitors already promise advice according to use, profession and budget.

Therefore IPCENTER differentiation cannot be only:
"tell us what you need and we recommend a laptop."

A stronger hypothesis is:
NEED -> SOFTWARE/WORKLOAD -> TECHNICAL REQUIREMENT -> EXACT MODELS -> LOCAL STOCK -> IMPORT OPTIONS -> CURRENT PRICE -> SUPPLIER -> WARRANTY -> TIME -> RISK -> QUOTATION -> PURCHASE -> SERIAL/PROVENANCE -> OUTCOME.

This remains a hypothesis to validate, not a final positioning decision.

## Adjacent-category evidence

### Drones
Public evidence shows:
- DJI official/authorized presence in Bolivia, including Santa Cruz and La Paz.

- repair/service/training as part of the market, not only hardware sales.
- public user interest in where to buy specific drone models and questions about training, investment, market demand, spares and maintenance.

Implication: a drone vertical is not simply import-on-demand; service, regulation, parts and technical support matter.

### Cellphones
Public conversations show demand for specific devices not easily obtained locally, but concerns about customs, shipping, warranty, seller trust and total cost.

### Auto parts
Public conversations show a distinction between common parts available locally and specific parts that require specialized sourcing/import. Compatibility and identifying what is actually demanded remain unresolved.

## Strategic implication for IPCENTER Passport
Current evidence strengthens, but does not yet prove, the value of a provenance/trust layer recording:
- exact model/variant
- seller/supplier evidence
- serial/IMEI/part number when relevant
- product condition
- quoted vs delivered configuration
- purchase evidence
- shipping/tracking
- receipt/inspection
- warranty terms
- delivery evidence

The value hypothesis is strongest where buyers fear fraud, substitutions, unclear warranty, refurbished/fake goods or remote purchasing.

## Evidence discipline
- Social comments are signals, not sales.
- Reddit/community posts are anecdotal and may be biased.
- Competitor marketing claims are claims, not independently verified service quality.
- Testimonials shown on a business's own site are self-published evidence and must not be treated as independent customer validation.
- Current and historical prices must remain separated.
- No market-share or population-prevalence claims are authorized from this pass.
