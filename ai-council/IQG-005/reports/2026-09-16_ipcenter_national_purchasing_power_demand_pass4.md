## Strategic correction
IPCenter must not model Bolivia as one homogeneous market or assume purchasing power from department averages. The useful unit is a layered geographic-commercial market:
CITY / MICROZONE / NEED / PRICE BAND / DIGITAL CHANNEL / FULFILLMENT / TRUST / PURCHASE OUTCOME.

No individual-level wealth inference should be made from social profiles. Purchasing-power analysis should use aggregate geographic evidence and explicit first-party transaction/budget data.

## New model: National Market Capacity Map
Do NOT build a single arbitrary 'richness score'. Build an evidence stack per city/microzone:

1. ECONOMIC_CAPACITY_PROXY
   - NBI / poverty indicators
   - household-income distribution where available
   - premium property-market evidence
   - formal economic/business density
   - high-value retail presence

2. DIGITAL_REACH
   - platform usage / discoverable communities
   - public groups/pages/accounts
   - WhatsApp business ecosystems

3. HIGH_TICKET_INTENT
   - explicit product requests
   - budget mentions
   - exact-model requests
   - software/use-case requests
   - import questions
   - price comparisons

4. VERIFIED_PURCHASE_BEHAVIOR
   - IPCENTER historical orders
   - quote-to-sale conversion
   - ticket
   - city
   - model/spec
   - payment method
   - repeat purchase

5. FULFILLMENT_FRICTION
   - shipping cost/time
   - warranty handling
   - trust concerns
   - product/variant verification

6. COMPETITIVE_INTENSITY
   - local stock
   - national sellers serving the city
   - price visibility
   - specialist competence

The useful question is not 'where are rich people?' but:
`WHERE does enough purchasing capacity intersect with a high-value unmet need that IPCENTER can fulfil better?`
