## Mission

Challenge whether IQ GROWTH's proposed Universal Market Intelligence Data Model can actually serve four very different live businesses without forcing them into the same workflow:

- IQG-002 VANSAM — restaurant / pizza + beverages
- IQG-003 Café Zacarías — farm + production + packaged coffee + channels
- IQG-004 Chocolates — packaged chocolate, brand pending
- IQG-005 IPCENTER — technology sourcing / procurement

The system must make each business easier to operate and grow, while preserving one reusable Core.

## Task 1 — Attack the universality claim

For every proposed universal field/process, classify:
- truly universal,
- reusable capability but vertical-specific semantics,
- should remain only in vertical adapter,
- unnecessary complexity.

Test against all four businesses.

## Task 2 — Minimum useful intelligence per vertical

For each business identify the minimum evidence needed to answer:
- who is the likely buyer/customer segment in commercial terms, without profiling individuals;
- what are they trying to buy/consume;
- where/how do they want to buy it;
- what blocks purchase;
- what evidence would justify a cheap test;
- what evidence would justify spending capital.

Do not use demographic stereotypes.

## Task 3 — "Magic" UX by vertical

Design the simplest customer/operator experience for:

VANSAM:
Customer wants food now. Reduce friction from menu discovery to order, prep, fulfillment and repeat.

Café Zacarías:
Customer/store/vendor wants coffee. Reduce friction around roast, format, price, freshness, channel and repeat purchase.

Chocolates:
Customer/store wants chocolate for self-consumption, gifting or resale. Reduce friction around product, cacao %, size, occasion, price and availability.

IPCenter:
Customer wants a machine/product and has money or financing path. Reduce friction around need, exact product fit, price, stock, warranty, sourcing and national delivery.

For each, state what the customer should NOT have to understand.

## Task 4 — Universal buyer-state challenge

Test whether:
AWARENESS -> DESIRE -> ACTIVE_SEARCH -> EXPLICIT_NEED -> BUDGET_DISCLOSED -> QUOTE -> PURCHASE -> FULFILLMENT -> OUTCOME -> REPEAT

works for all verticals.

For restaurant impulse purchases and low-ticket products, propose a better event model if needed.

## Task 5 — Cross-vertical Market Memory

Design what Market Memory should remember without becoming a surveillance dossier.

Separate:
- market-level memory,
- product-level memory,
- city/microzone memory,
- anonymous/aggregated behavior,
- first-party customer history.

What should expire? What should remain historical forever? What must never be linked?

## Task 6 — Action ladder

Challenge:
WEAK SIGNAL -> RESEARCH
CORROBORATED SIGNAL -> LOW-COST TEST
FIRST-PARTY INQUIRY -> OFFER/QUOTE
ACCEPTED OFFER -> PREPARE/SOURCE
PAYMENT -> FULFILL
REPEATED PROFITABLE OUTCOME -> SCALE

Show where this fails in restaurant, packaged food and technology.

## Task 7 — Social surfaces as sensors

For Facebook, TikTok, Instagram, WhatsApp, reviews, marketplaces, delivery platforms and human observation:
- what can each source legitimately tell us;
- what can it NOT tell us;
- what source is best for discovery, validation, conversion and outcome per vertical.

Do not assume a platform API exists.

## Task 8 — Cross-vertical experiments

Design at least 5 cheap falsifiable experiments per business.

Examples of experiment families:
- content test,
- product availability test,
- price/offer framing,
- channel test,
- bundle/combo,
- sampling,
- preorder,
- delivery radius,
- seller route,
- exact product sourcing.

No arbitrary success thresholds. Mark `TO_CALIBRATE`.

Find at least 20 things that should never be forced into one generic schema/process because doing so would damage the business.
